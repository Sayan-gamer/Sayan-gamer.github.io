Cold Storage Design

The basic purpose of cold storage is to store perishable food products at an optimum temperature to enhance the shelf-life of the products.

The storage conditions to be maintained as well as the method of storage for these cold storages vary depending on the optimum storage conditions required for different products.

Types of Cold Storage

Based on Operating Temperature

  • Above 0 ºC: Used for milk, fruits, and vegetables.
Produce Storage (Above 0 ºC)
  • Below 0 ºC: Used for ice-cream, meat, fish, etc.
Freezer Storage (Below 0 ºC)

Based on Construction

  • Constructed cold storage: Permanent construction.
Constructed Cold Storage
  • Walk-in cold storage: Assembled units.
Walk-in Cold Storage

Loads in Cold Storages

To accurately design a cold storage system, various heat loads must be accounted for:

  • Transmission loads (t): Heat transferred through walls, roof, and floor.
  • Infiltration load (i): Heat from outside air entering the space.
Loads in Cold Storages

Product Load

  • Chilling load (c): Heat removed to lower product temperature above freezing.
  • Freezing load (f): Latent heat removed to freeze the product.
  • Cooling load before freezing (cb): Sensible heat removed just prior to the phase change.
  • Heat generated (m): Heat generated by the biological respiration of the material to be stored.
Product Load

Internal Loads

  • Electrical Appliances (e): Cooling load required to offset heat from internal lighting and electronics.
  • Equipment (eq): Cooling load required for fans, motors, and similar operational equipment.
Internal Loads

Calculation of Cooling Loads

Total heat load = t + i + c + f + cb + m + e + eq

Definition: The quantity of heat energy that needs to be extracted from a space to keep it cool and comfortable (or at the required freezing/chilling threshold) regardless of internal or external heat generation.
